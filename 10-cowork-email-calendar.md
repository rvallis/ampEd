## 🧠 THE WHAT

# LET IT HANDLE THE ROUTINE PART.

Six words. Once Gmail and Calendar are connected, Claude can draft, summarize, and check availability for you. Your job shifts to reviewing and deciding, not doing every step by hand.

### The One-Sentence Version

> A scheduling exchange that normally takes several emails back and forth can often become one message, if Claude checks your real calendar and drafts the reply for you to review.

### The Core Idea

Scheduling friction is a real, measurable cost, a typical back-and-forth to lock in a meeting time can take three to five separate emails, and each exchange adds mental load even when any single message seems small. Multiply that across a dozen client relationships and it becomes hours of pure coordination work every week, time that isn't billable and isn't the actual job you're good at.

With Gmail and Calendar connected, Claude can draft a reply that already checks your real availability, summarize a long thread so you don't have to reread it from the top, or flag what's still outstanding across your inbox. You review and send, Claude does the retrieving and drafting.

**Analogy:** think of a good executive assistant. They don't make your decisions for you, but they draft the reply, check the calendar, and hand you something ready to approve, instead of you starting from a blank page every time. That's the role Claude plays here, not a replacement for your judgment, a draft ready for it.

### What to Hand Off vs. What to Keep

| Hand off to Claude | Keep for yourself |
|---|---|
| Drafting a scheduling reply based on real availability | Final tone check before anything sensitive gets sent |
| Summarizing a long thread you're behind on | Any actual decision about a client relationship |
| Flagging what's still outstanding in your inbox | Sending, always review before it goes out |

### Quick Check

A client emails asking for three possible meeting times next week. What's the efficient way to handle this with Claude?

<details>
<summary><strong>See a strong answer</strong></summary>

> Ask Claude to check your actual calendar for open slots next week and draft a reply offering three specific times. You review the draft, adjust if needed, and send. That's one round trip instead of the usual three-to-five-email back-and-forth of proposing, getting told those don't work, and re-proposing.

The efficient version isn't "let Claude send it automatically." It's "let Claude do the checking and drafting, so your only job is a quick review."

## 🔄 THE HOW

---

### "One Message Instead of a Five-Email Chain"
**For freelancers and consultants managing multiple clients**

A consultant used to spend real time on the back-and-forth of finding a meeting slot: propose times, get told none work, check calendar again, propose again. Now she asks Claude to check her actual availability and draft a reply with real open times before she even opens the thread. What used to take several email exchanges over a day or two often resolves in one round trip.

**The scheduling problem was never that she couldn't find open time. It was the repeated cost of the back-and-forth required to communicate it.**

*Research note: a typical scheduling exchange requiring three to five separate emails, and the real mental burden each exchange adds, is a documented, named cost of freelance and small-business coordination work, not just an annoyance.*

---

### "Catching Up on a Thread Without Rereading the Whole Thing"
**For anyone returning from time away from their inbox**

A small business owner coming back from a few days away used to dread a cluttered inbox, not because she couldn't handle it, but because catching up meant rereading long threads from the top just to remember where things stood. Asking Claude to summarize what's happened in a specific thread while she was out let her get back up to speed in a minute instead of twenty.

**She wasn't avoiding the work. She was avoiding the re-reading required just to know what the work even was.**

---

### "The Standing Meeting That Runs Itself Now"
**For anyone coordinating recurring check-ins with clients or a team**

Someone managing several ongoing client relationships used to manually track who was due for a check-in call and when. Now she can ask Claude, working from the actual calendar, which clients haven't had a check-in scheduled in the last month, and draft outreach for each one. The tracking that used to live in her head, imperfectly, now lives somewhere she can actually query.

**The relationships still need her. The remembering-to-check-in part didn't.**

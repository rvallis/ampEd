## 🧠 THE WHAT

# HAND OVER THE FOLDER, NOT THE SUMMARY.

Six words. Cowork can work directly with a folder of real files, not just what you describe about them. Give it the actual documents, and let it do the reading and assembling.

### The One-Sentence Version

> If a task starts with "go through this pile of files and pull out X," that's exactly the kind of work Cowork is built to do directly, instead of you doing the reading yourself first.

### The Core Idea

A lot of real business admin work is fundamentally file-assembly: turn a folder of receipts into an expense report, turn a stack of client notes into a status summary, turn scattered drafts into one finished document. Doing this by hand means you're the one reading every file, one at a time, before any actual synthesis happens.

Cowork can be pointed at a folder and given a task like "read every receipt in here and build an expense report" directly. You're not retyping information out of documents, you're describing the end result you want and letting Claude do the reading.

**Analogy:** think of the difference between telling someone "the invoices are somewhere in this office, good luck," versus handing them a labeled folder and saying "these are this month's invoices, total them by category." One requires them to become a detective first. The other lets them just do the actual job.

### What This Looks Like in Practice

| Starting point | What you ask for |
|---|---|
| A folder of receipt photos | "Build an expense report categorized by type, with a total" |
| A folder of scattered client notes | "Summarize the current status of each client into one document" |
| Several draft versions of the same document | "Compare these and tell me what's different, then merge the best of each" |

### Quick Check

You have a folder of twenty PDF invoices from different vendors and need a single spreadsheet totaling spend by vendor. What's the efficient move?

## 🔄 THE HOW

---

### "The Expense Report That Used to Eat an Entire Afternoon"
**For freelancers and consultants managing their own business admin**

A freelance consultant used to dread expense report season, a folder of crumpled receipt photos she'd manually sort, categorize, and total by hand, usually putting it off until the deadline forced her. Pointing Cowork at the folder directly and asking for a categorized expense report with totals turned an afternoon of tedious admin into a task she could kick off and review, rather than one she had to personally execute line by line.

**The receipts didn't change. What changed was who was doing the reading.**

---

### "Turning Scattered Client Notes Into One Real Status Update"
**For anyone managing multiple ongoing projects**

Someone running several client engagements at once used to keep notes scattered across separate documents, one per meeting, with no single place showing where each client actually stood. Pointing Cowork at the whole folder of notes and asking for a current status summary per client, built directly from the real notes rather than her memory of them, gave her something she could actually hand to a client or use to prep for a call.

**She wasn't missing the information. It was just scattered across a dozen files instead of living in one usable place.**

---

### "Merging Three Half-Finished Drafts Into One Good One"
**For anyone building instructional or training materials for others**

Someone building a training module had three different draft versions floating around, each with something worth keeping and something worth cutting. Instead of manually comparing them line by line, pointing Cowork at all three files and asking it to compare and merge the strongest parts of each produced a working synthesis in minutes rather than the hour of side-by-side reading it would have taken by hand.

**The good material already existed across those three drafts. The bottleneck was the manual comparison, not the writing.**

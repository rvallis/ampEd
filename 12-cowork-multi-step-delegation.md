## 🧠 THE WHAT

# ONE INSTRUCTION, MANY STEPS.

Five words. Cowork can carry out a task that involves several steps, searching, reading, drafting, from a single instruction, checking back in when it needs your input, not after every micro-step.

### The One-Sentence Version

> Instead of directing Claude through a task one small step at a time, describe the full outcome you want and let Claude figure out and execute the steps to get there.

### The Core Idea

A regular Claude chat conversation is naturally one exchange at a time, you ask, it answers, you ask again. Cowork is built for a different shape of work: describe a multi-step outcome, "find every email from this client, summarize what's outstanding, and draft a follow-up," and Claude works through the whole chain, searching, reading, summarizing, and drafting, without you having to direct each individual step.

This is a real shift in how you think about instructions. Instead of "step one, do this," "okay now step two," you're describing the destination and letting Claude find the route.

**Analogy:** think of the difference between micromanaging a task, "first open the file, now scroll to page three, now copy that paragraph," versus delegating it, "pull together everything relevant from this client and draft a summary." The second version trusts the person, or in this case Claude, to handle the steps in between, and only checking in with you when something genuinely needs your input.

### An Important Caution

When you delegate a multi-step task, be as clear and unambiguous as possible about what you actually want, since Claude will be taking a string of actions without checking in after each one. A vague or contradictory instruction given this kind of autonomy is riskier than the same vagueness in a single back-and-forth chat message, because there's more room for it to compound across several steps before you see the result.

### Quick Check

You want Claude to review every email from a specific client this month and draft a summary of open items. Should you break this into several small Claude Chat requests, or delegate it as one Cowork task?

## 🔄 THE HOW

---

### "Describing the Outcome Instead of Managing Every Step"
**For freelancers and consultants managing multiple clients**

A consultant used to work through client email catch-up step by step in chat: "find emails from this client," then review the results, then "summarize these," then "now draft a follow-up based on that summary," each step a separate exchange. Delegating the whole thing as one instruction, "find this month's emails from this client, summarize what's outstanding, draft a follow-up," got her the same finished result without her having to manage the handoff between each step herself.

**The work didn't get smaller. The number of times she had to personally direct it did.**

---

### "Trusting the Chain, With One Real Guardrail"
**For anyone new to handing off multi-step work**

Someone new to Cowork initially gave vague instructions, "handle my client intake," and got results that didn't match what she actually meant, since Claude had to guess at what "handle" meant without her checking in along the way. The fix wasn't abandoning delegation, it was being more specific up front: exactly what to extract, how to validate it, when to flag something for her rather than guess. The more autonomous the task, the more that upfront clarity actually matters.

**Delegation doesn't mean vagueness is fine. It means clarity has to happen once, up front, instead of getting corrected step by step.**

---

### "The Same Instinct as Delegating to a New Employee"
**For teachers thinking about management, or managers thinking about teaching**

A manager compares this directly to delegating a real project to a capable new employee: you don't stand over their shoulder directing every micro-step, you describe the outcome clearly, trust them to figure out the steps, and check in at the end or when they hit something genuinely uncertain. Multi-step delegation with Claude works the same way, and the skill of writing a clear, complete instruction up front is identical to the skill of writing a clear project brief for a person.

**The difference between good delegation and micromanaging isn't how much gets handed off. It's how clearly the outcome was described before you let go of the steps.**

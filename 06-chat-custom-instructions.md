## 🧠 THE WHAT

# SAY IT ONCE. NOT EVERY TIME.

Six words. Somewhere in your account settings, there's a place to tell Claude who you are and what you generally need, once, so you're not retyping it at the start of every new conversation.

### The One-Sentence Version

> Every fresh Claude conversation starts with no memory of the last one, unless you've told it, once, in settings, the kind of context you'd otherwise be repeating every single time.

### The Core Idea

Repeating the same context to an AI tool over and over is a widely reported productivity drain, most people who use tools like Claude regularly hit this exact wall: type out your role, your grade level, your typical needs, get help, close the tab, open a new conversation next week, type it all out again.

Custom instructions, usually found in account or profile settings, exist specifically to fix this. Write a short description of who you are and what you generally need once, and it applies automatically going forward, without you thinking about it in each new conversation.

**Analogy:** think about the difference between a substitute teacher who gets handed a fresh packet of "here's my class, here's how I run things" every single time they sub for you, versus a regular long-term sub who already knows your room. The first is fine once. Doing it every single time is exhausting and unnecessary. Custom instructions are how you turn every new Claude conversation into the second kind.

### What Belongs in Custom Instructions (and What Doesn't)

| Good fit | Poor fit |
|---|---|
| Your role ("I teach 7th grade math and PE") | Details specific to one single task, right now |
| General preferences ("I prefer concise answers, no fluff") | Anything sensitive you wouldn't want stored long-term |
| Standing context that's true in most conversations | One-off context that only matters for today's request |

### Quick Check

You're a teacher who always wants lesson materials pitched at a 7th grade reading level unless you say otherwise. Where does that belong, custom instructions, or something you type fresh each time?

<details>
<summary><strong>See a strong answer</strong></summary>

> Custom instructions. It's true across almost every conversation, not specific to one request, exactly the kind of standing context that's a productivity drain to keep retyping. You'd still mention a grade-level override in a specific message if one particular request needed something different, but the default belongs in settings, said once.

The test is simple: if you find yourself typing the same sentence at the start of conversation after conversation, it belongs in custom instructions instead.

</details>

## 🔄 THE HOW

---

### "The Sentence She Was Typing Every Single Monday"
**For classroom teachers, any grade level**

A teacher noticed she was typing some version of the same sentence at the start of almost every Claude conversation: "I teach 7th grade math, keep things age-appropriate and not overly formal." Once she put that in her custom instructions, every new conversation just already knew it, no more re-explaining her grade level and tone preference before she could even get to her actual question.

**It wasn't a big change. It just removed thirty seconds of repeated typing from the start of every single conversation, which adds up fast across a school year.**

*Research note: repeating the same context at the start of every AI conversation is a widely documented productivity drain, and setting up custom instructions once is specifically named as the standard, minimum-effort fix.*

---

### "Not Explaining Her Business From Scratch, Every Time"
**For freelancers, consultants, and small business owners**

A freelance instructional designer used to open every new Claude conversation with a paragraph explaining her business, her typical clients, and her preferred tone for client-facing writing. Moving that into custom instructions meant every new conversation started already knowing the basics, freeing her first message to be about the actual task instead of background.

**She wasn't giving Claude less information. She was giving it once instead of constantly.**

---

### "Setting Expectations Once, Not Every Time You Meet"
**For teachers thinking about management, or managers thinking about teaching**

A manager compared it to onboarding: you don't re-explain your team's communication norms to the same employee every single week, you set expectations once, clearly, and then everyone operates from that shared understanding going forward. Custom instructions do the same job for Claude, a one-time setup that quietly shapes every future conversation, instead of a rule you have to restate constantly.

**The frustrating part was never explaining herself. It was having to explain herself again and again, when once should have been enough.**
